# Final manuscript reviewer audit

## Target journal requirements checked

The final manuscript was checked against the current Elsevier Computers in Industry author guidance and journal scope. The journal requires a concise abstract of no more than 250 words, 1-7 English keywords, and 3-5 highlights with no more than 85 characters per bullet. The journal also emphasizes original, high-quality, application-oriented research in industrial computing. Graphical abstracts are encouraged, and data availability disclosure is expected.

## Compliance result

- Abstract length: 205 words, within the 250-word limit.
- Keywords: 7 keywords, within the required range.
- Highlights: 5 bullets, all within the 85-character limit.
- Graphical abstract: Fig. 1 is included and captioned.
- Main manuscript structure: title, highlights, abstract, keywords, Introduction, Related work, Problem formulation, Method, Experimental design, Results, Discussion, Conclusion, figure captions, supplementary captions, data availability, code availability, AI declaration, competing interest statement, CRediT statement, acknowledgements, references.
- Journal fit: the manuscript is framed as application-oriented industrial decision support, not as purely algorithmic model comparison.
- Claim boundary: no autonomous process control, defect reduction, external-site validation, or universal superiority claim is made.

## Reviewer-style judgement

The manuscript is suitable as a full working draft for a Computers in Industry submission under the stated retrospective single-database boundary. It links an industrial SMT first-article problem to a concrete computing framework, integrates case-based reasoning, structural optimal transport, selective review, and uncertainty reporting, and provides source-data-backed figures and tables. The strongest remaining limitation is not writing structure but evidence scope: prospective engineer-supervised validation and external-site validation remain future work.

The final author list, affiliations, funding details, institutional approvals, controlled-access data route, and exact CRediT assignments must be completed by the authors before submission because they cannot be inferred from the current workspace.
